# SkinCancer.org: Sunscreen & Your Skin
Source: https://www.skincancer.org/skin-cancer-prevention/sun-protection/sunscreen/

## Why It Matters
Regular daily use of SPF 15 sunscreen reduces squamous cell carcinoma risk by ~40% and melanoma risk by ~50%.

## The 5 W's of Sunscreen
- WHO: everyone over 6 months old.
- WHAT: broad-spectrum SPF 30+, or SPF 50+ for extended outdoor time.
- WHEN: daily, 30 minutes before going outside, then every 2 hours.
- WHERE: all exposed skin — ears, neck, scalp part line, tops of feet, behind knees are commonly missed.
- HOW: about 1 ounce (shot glass) for full body coverage.

## SPF Levels
- SPF 30: daily use, occasional/incidental sun exposure.
- SPF 50+: extended outdoor activity — hiking, running, swimming, sports.

## Ingredients
- Mineral (titanium dioxide, zinc oxide): sit on skin surface, mostly absorb/reflect UV, generally lower irritation.
- Chemical (e.g. avobenzone, bemotrizinol): absorb UV and dissipate it as heat.
Both types are tested safe and effective when used as directed.

## Water Resistance
No sunscreen is fully waterproof. "Water resistant" lasts up to 40 minutes in water; "very water resistant" up to 80 minutes — reapply after.

## Beyond Sunscreen
Combine sunscreen with shade, protective clothing, wide-brimmed hats, and UV-blocking sunglasses for full protection. Up to 80% of UV still reaches skin on cloudy days.
