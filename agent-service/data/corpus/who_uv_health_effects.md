# WHO: Known Health Effects of Ultraviolet Radiation
Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/radiation-the-known-health-effects-of-ultraviolet-radiation

## UV-A and UV-B
UV-A penetrates deep into skin (dermis), causing gradual elasticity loss and premature aging. UV-B is absorbed in the epidermis and is the primary cause of sunburn, and significantly increases skin cancer risk even without visible sunburn.

## Suntan and Sunburn
There is no healthy tan. A tan offers an SPF of only 2-4 and represents skin damage, not protection. Sunburn is UV-B-triggered inflammation peaking 12-36 hours after exposure. Frequent childhood/adolescent sunburns substantially raise melanoma risk later in life.

## Skin Cancer Types
- Basal cell carcinoma: most common, slow-growing, rarely spreads.
- Squamous cell carcinoma: second most common, can occasionally metastasize.
- Melanoma: least common but most dangerous, responsible for most skin cancer deaths. Early detection greatly improves survival.

## Vitamin D
UV-B stimulates vitamin D synthesis. Adequate levels require exposing face/hands/arms without sunscreen 2-3 times a week for about half the time it would normally take to sunburn (roughly 12 minutes for sensitive skin). This should be balanced against cumulative UV damage risk.

## Dark Skin
Darker skin has lower but nonzero melanoma/skin-cancer risk. Regardless of skin colour, UV still causes DNA damage, skin aging, eye damage, and immune suppression.
